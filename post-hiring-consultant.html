Hiring a software consultant: what to look for

Mar 18, 2026

The consulting industry has a reputation problem that isn't entirely undeserved. There are consultants who charge a lot, produce polished decks, and leave you no better off than before they arrived. Avoiding that outcome comes down to a few things you can assess before you sign anything.

Do they ask more questions than they answer?

A consultant who spends the first conversation mostly talking about their own experience is a yellow flag. The best consultants are obsessive about understanding your situation before offering anything. They ask about your constraints, your history, the things you've already tried. They're curious in a way that's almost uncomfortable.

Do they tell you things you don't want to hear?

The value of an outside perspective is that it should be honest in a way that internal voices sometimes can't be. If a consultant agrees with everything you say, they're not doing their job. Look for someone who will push back — not contrarily, but specifically. "Here's what concerns me about that approach, and here's why" is a good sign.

Can they explain things simply?

Technical complexity is not inherently impressive. Consultants who can only talk about problems in technical terms — and can't connect their work to business outcomes — tend to produce solutions that satisfy engineers but don't move the needle. Look for someone who can make their work legible to non-technical stakeholders.

Are they clear about what they don't know?

Overconfidence is a bigger risk in consulting than most clients realize. The best consultants are precise about the boundaries of their knowledge. "I'm not familiar with that specific compliance framework, but here's how I'd approach getting up to speed" is a much better answer than false certainty.

What does success look like, specifically?

Before any engagement starts, you should have a clear, written definition of what success looks like. Not "improved system performance" but "response times under 200ms for 95th percentile requests." Not "better architecture" but "new services can be deployed independently without coordinating with the platform team." Consultants who resist this kind of specificity are telling you something.
