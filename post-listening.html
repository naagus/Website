Consulting Strategy

The underrated skill in software consulting: listening

Feb 10, 2026

We've worked with a lot of engineers over the years, and the ones who become great consultants all share something in common: they're exceptionally good at getting other people to talk.

This sounds obvious. It's not, in practice.

The instinct to solve

Most engineers are trained to move quickly from problem to solution. You hear a problem, you understand it, you propose a fix. This is exactly the right instinct for well-defined technical problems. But it's a liability in consulting, where the stated problem is often not the real problem.

We've learned to slow down when a client says something like "our deployment pipeline is too slow." That might be literally true. But it might also mean: "we don't trust our CI/CD and we're adding manual gates to compensate." Or "our branching strategy creates merge conflicts that slow everything down." Or "our infrastructure team and application team have different incentives and can't agree on a solution."

The fix for a slow pipeline and the fix for misaligned incentives are very different engagements.

How to actually listen

Good listening in consulting looks like:

  • Asking "how does this affect you specifically?" before jumping to solutions
  • Following up on throwaway comments ("you mentioned you tried X before — what happened?")
  • Repeating back what you've heard before proposing anything ("let me make sure I understand the situation…")
  • Being comfortable with silence — letting a question sit instead of answering it yourself

Why it matters beyond diagnosis

Getting the diagnosis right is the obvious reason to listen well. But there's a second, less obvious reason: people implement solutions they feel ownership over. When you've listened carefully enough to reflect someone's problem back to them in their own terms, they start to see your proposed solution as a collaboration rather than an imposition.

The consultants who get the most done are the ones who've made their clients feel like the solution was their idea.
